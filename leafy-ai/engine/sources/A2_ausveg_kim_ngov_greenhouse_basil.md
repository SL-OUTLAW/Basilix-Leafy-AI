# Veg Grower Kim Ngov in-focus - Greenhouse Basil

## Greenhouse basil production

Kim Ngov grows basil in a greenhouse throughout the year.

The greenhouse production system allows basil to be produced across different seasons.

A heating system is used to maintain suitable temperatures for basil production.

## Temperature-responsive watering

The greenhouse uses a watering system that considers both time and temperature rather than relying only on a fixed time schedule.

When weather conditions are hotter, the crop receives water more frequently.

On cooler days, the crop receives water less frequently but for longer watering periods.

This approach links irrigation frequency with changing environmental conditions.

## Growing medium

Kim Ngov has moved from growing cucumbers and tomatoes in the greenhouse to growing basil.

The basil is grown in peat moss.

The grower reported that growing basil in peat moss uses less fertiliser and allows the crop to grow faster.

## Commercial production

The greenhouse basil operation is part of a diversified vegetable business.

The grower considers production efficiency and the relationship between inputs and crop yield important.

The aim is to maximise the return obtained from production inputs rather than simply increasing the number of plants.

The grower described greenhouse basil as a successful part of the business.

## Sustainable production practices

The grower is also experimenting with sustainable production practices in outdoor crops.

These include reducing the use of plastic mulch and using cover-crop residues as mulch.

Cover crops are being investigated for their potential to improve soil structure, water infiltration and weed management.

The farm has also moved away from raised beds in some areas and developed permanent tractor rows.

## Crop management and experimentation

The grower describes experimentation with different management techniques as an important part of the farm's development.

The approach involves testing production practices and observing their effects on crop health, inputs and profitability.

The grower aims to reduce unnecessary inputs such as fertiliser and labour while maintaining productive crops.
