# Sensor-Based Nutrient Management

## Sensor-based crop management

Sensors can be used to monitor crop and growing-environment conditions in controlled production systems.

Sensor measurements can provide information that supports irrigation, nutrient management and environmental control.

Monitoring systems can use sensor data to identify changes in crop conditions and support timely management decisions.

## Important measurements

Measurements used in sensor-based nutrient management can include electrical conductivity, pH, water content, irrigation volume and environmental conditions.

Electrical conductivity can provide an indication of the concentration of dissolved salts in the root environment.

pH provides information about the acidity or alkalinity of the nutrient solution or growing medium.

These measurements can be used together with information about crop growth and irrigation to evaluate nutrient-management conditions.

## Irrigation monitoring

Sensor-based irrigation can help provide water according to crop demand.

Monitoring water availability can reduce unnecessary irrigation and help maintain suitable root-zone conditions.

Irrigation decisions should consider the characteristics of the growing medium, crop growth stage, environmental conditions and water availability.

## Nutrient management

Nutrient management can be improved by combining sensor measurements with information about crop growth and nutrient-solution conditions.

Electrical conductivity can be used as a general indicator of salt concentration, but it does not identify individual nutrients.

Nutrient concentrations should therefore be evaluated using appropriate nutrient analyses when detailed nutrient information is required.

## Automation

Sensors can be connected to control systems to automate some production decisions.

Automated systems can respond to sensor measurements according to predefined rules.

Automation can be used to support irrigation and nutrient management while reducing the need for continuous manual intervention.

Sensor-based control should include appropriate limits and safeguards to prevent unsuitable operating conditions.

## Sensor calibration

Sensors should be calibrated and maintained so that their measurements remain reliable.

Incorrect sensor readings can lead to inappropriate irrigation or nutrient-management decisions.

Calibration procedures depend on the type of sensor and the measurement being performed.

Regular checks can help identify sensor drift or malfunction.

## Data interpretation

Sensor values should be interpreted in the context of crop stage and environmental conditions.

A change in EC or pH does not necessarily identify the exact cause of a plant response.

Sensor data should be combined with observations of plant growth, irrigation conditions and other relevant measurements.

## Root-zone monitoring

Root-zone monitoring can provide information about water availability and nutrient conditions around plant roots.

Monitoring can help identify periods of excessive drying, excessive moisture or changing salt concentrations.

Maintaining suitable root-zone conditions is important for healthy crop development.

## Decision support

Sensor data can support decision-making by providing continuous or repeated measurements.

Decision rules can be developed for irrigation or other crop-management actions.

Automated decisions should be based on validated thresholds and should include safety limits.

## Relevance to Leafy AI
