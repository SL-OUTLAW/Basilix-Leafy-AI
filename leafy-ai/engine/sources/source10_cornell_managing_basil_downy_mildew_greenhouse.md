# Managing Basil Downy Mildew in the Greenhouse

## Disease development

Basil downy mildew is a disease caused by the oomycete Peronospora belbahrii.

The disease can develop rapidly under favourable environmental conditions.

Warm temperatures combined with high humidity can favour disease development.

The pathogen produces spores that can spread through air movement.

## Symptoms

Initial symptoms commonly appear as yellowing on the upper surface of basil leaves.

As the disease progresses, the affected areas can expand.

A gray to purple, downy growth may develop on the underside of affected leaves.

Severely affected leaves can become brown and collapse.

Symptoms can reduce the quality and marketability of basil.

## Humidity and leaf wetness

High relative humidity increases the risk of basil downy mildew.

Extended periods of leaf wetness can create conditions favourable for infection.

Managing humidity and reducing prolonged leaf wetness are important parts of greenhouse disease management.

Good airflow can help reduce humidity around the foliage.

## Air movement

Adequate air movement through the crop can improve drying of foliage and reduce periods of high humidity.

Air circulation can also improve environmental uniformity within a greenhouse.

Fans and appropriate greenhouse ventilation can be used to improve air movement.

Air movement should be considered together with temperature and humidity management.

## Irrigation practices

Irrigation practices can influence leaf wetness and greenhouse humidity.

Avoiding unnecessary overhead watering can reduce the amount of water remaining on leaf surfaces.

Water should be managed to provide adequate crop requirements while limiting prolonged periods of foliage wetness.

## Lighting and disease management

Adequate lighting supports healthy basil growth and can contribute to an environment that is less favourable to disease development.

Greenhouse lighting should therefore be considered together with humidity, airflow and temperature management.

Lighting alone does not prevent downy mildew.

## Monitoring

Regular crop monitoring is important because early symptoms can be difficult to distinguish from some nutritional disorders.

Leaves should be inspected for yellowing and the characteristic downy growth on the underside.

Environmental conditions should also be monitored, particularly relative humidity and periods of prolonged leaf wetness.

Early recognition allows management measures to be implemented before disease becomes widespread.

## Resistant varieties

Use of basil varieties with resistance to downy mildew can reduce disease risk.

Resistance is not necessarily complete and should be considered as one component of an integrated disease-management approach.

## Greenhouse management

An integrated approach can include resistant varieties, environmental management, crop monitoring and appropriate sanitation practices.

Reducing humidity, improving airflow and limiting leaf wetness are important environmental-management strategies.

The greenhouse should be managed to avoid prolonged periods of conditions that favour pathogen development.
