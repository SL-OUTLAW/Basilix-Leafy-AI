# Controlled Environment Agriculture - Crop Resources

## Controlled-environment agriculture

Controlled-environment agriculture uses protected production systems to manage environmental conditions for crop growth.

The production environment can be managed through control of factors such as light, temperature, humidity, carbon dioxide, water and nutrients.

Controlled environments can help provide more consistent crop conditions than open-field production.

## Crop selection

Different crops have different environmental requirements.

Crop selection for controlled environments should consider growth rate, plant size, light requirements, temperature response, nutrient demand and market characteristics.

Leafy greens and herbs are examples of crops that can be produced in controlled environments.

## Lighting

Light is essential for photosynthesis and crop development.

Controlled-environment systems may use natural light, supplemental electric lighting or a combination of both.

The amount, duration and distribution of light can influence plant growth and crop quality.

Plant-level light measurements can support decisions about supplemental lighting.

## Temperature

Temperature affects photosynthesis, respiration, nutrient uptake and overall crop development.

Controlled environments can use heating and cooling systems to maintain suitable temperature conditions.

Day and night temperatures may be managed differently depending on crop requirements.

## Humidity

Relative humidity affects transpiration and plant water balance.

High humidity can increase disease risk, particularly when foliage remains wet for extended periods.

Air movement and ventilation can help manage humidity within protected growing environments.

## Carbon dioxide

Carbon dioxide is required for photosynthesis.

Controlled environments may monitor or supplement carbon dioxide concentrations depending on the crop and production system.

The benefit of carbon dioxide enrichment depends on other environmental factors, particularly light availability.

## Hydroponic crop production

Hydroponic systems supply water and mineral nutrients directly to plant roots.

Different hydroponic systems provide different methods of supporting plants and delivering nutrient solution.

Examples include nutrient film technique, deep-water culture and substrate-based systems.

## Nutrient management

Plant nutrient requirements vary between crops and growth stages.

Hydroponic nutrient solutions should provide essential mineral nutrients in appropriate proportions.

Nutrient-solution pH and electrical conductivity are commonly monitored.

Electrical conductivity provides an indication of total dissolved salts but does not identify individual nutrients.

Water quality should be considered when preparing and managing nutrient solutions.

## Water management

Controlled-environment crops require appropriate water delivery.

Irrigation practices depend on crop requirements, growing medium, environmental conditions and system design.

Recirculating hydroponic systems can improve water-use efficiency but require monitoring of nutrient and water conditions.

## Environmental monitoring

Sensors can be used to monitor environmental conditions.

Common measurements include air temperature, relative humidity, light, carbon dioxide, water temperature, pH and electrical conductivity.

Monitoring data can support automation and management decisions.

Sensor measurements should be interpreted in context because a single measurement does not necessarily identify the cause of a crop response.

## Plant health

Controlled environments can reduce exposure to some external stresses but do not eliminate pests or diseases.

Plant health should be monitored regularly.

Changes in leaf appearance, growth rate, root condition or environmental measurements may indicate a developing problem.

Early detection can help reduce the impact of plant-health problems.

## Basil and leafy greens

Basil and other leafy greens can be grown under controlled conditions.

The environmental requirements of basil should be considered when selecting lighting, temperature, humidity and nutrient-management strategies.

Crop-specific sources should be used when exact basil production values are required.
