# Protected Cropping in Action - Australian Basil and Soft Herbs







## Protected cropping in Australian horticulture

Protected cropping has traditionally been considered a niche sector compared with conventional growing methods.

Protected cropping can require significant investment, including glasshouses, irrigation and nutrient systems, and management of environmental conditions to create suitable growing environments.

Butler Market Gardens operates 12 cropping sites and supplies fresh herbs and vegetables to major retailers.

Its Lyndhurst operation near Melbourne predominantly grows herbs in approximately 3000 square metres of protected cropping space.

The facility was historically a flower farm and was converted for herb production as demand for herbs and different retail formats increased.

The operation uses winter heating supported by an environmentally friendly biomass boiler.

## Benefits of protected cropping

Protected cropping can allow growers to produce crops using a smaller amount of land.

The Lyndhurst operation is used to produce fresh products close to consumers around Melbourne.

The grower reported that some products can be turned around in approximately four weeks, with a record of 18 days from planting to harvest while meeting customer specifications.

Protected cropping is increasingly being used for leafy green and other short shelf-life products that benefit from being produced close to consumers.

## YieldX high-density growing trial

Since November 2025, the Lyndhurst greenhouse has hosted a commercial trial of a high-density growing system developed by YieldX.

The system was designed to make greater use of unused vertical and horizontal space within a glasshouse and increase production capacity.

The technology was originally engineered for NASA's deep-space food program.

The YieldX system changes its growing space as plants increase in size.

As plants grow, the available space is adjusted to accommodate plant size while allowing greater plant density.

## Basil production context

The Lyndhurst operation includes a 1000 square metre glasshouse that predominantly grows basil and soft herbs.

The commercial trial aims to maximise the amount of product that can be produced within the available glasshouse space.

The grower identified winter production as an important period for maximising production density.

The high-density system is intended to increase the amount of product that can be produced within the same protected cropping area.

## Labour efficiency

The YieldX system was also introduced to improve labour efficiency.

Walking long distances along rows can increase the time required to complete production tasks.

Increasing production volume while controlling labour costs was identified as an important consideration for long-term commercial viability.

The grower reported that labour can represent nearly half of many farmers' production costs.
