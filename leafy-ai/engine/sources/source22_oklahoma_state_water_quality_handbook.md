# Water Quality Handbook for Nurseries

## Water quality in crop production

Water quality is an important factor in plant production because irrigation water becomes part of the plant growing environment.

The characteristics of source water can affect nutrient management, irrigation practices and plant growth.

Important water-quality properties include pH, electrical conductivity, alkalinity and the concentration of dissolved minerals.

## Electrical conductivity

Electrical conductivity is an indicator of the concentration of dissolved salts in water.

Water with high EC contains greater concentrations of dissolved salts.

High concentrations of soluble salts can increase the salinity of the root environment.

Plant response to salinity depends on the crop and the concentration of salts present.

EC measurements can therefore be useful when evaluating irrigation-water quality.

## pH

Water pH describes the acidity or alkalinity of the water.

Water pH can influence nutrient availability and the effectiveness of nutrient-management programs.

The pH of irrigation water should be considered together with alkalinity and the composition of the nutrient solution.

## Alkalinity

Alkalinity represents the capacity of water to neutralise acids.

High alkalinity can cause the pH of growing media or nutrient solutions to increase over time.

Water alkalinity is therefore important when selecting and managing irrigation-water sources.

Acidification may sometimes be used to reduce the effect of high alkalinity, but treatment requirements depend on the water characteristics and production system.

## Dissolved minerals

Irrigation water can contain calcium, magnesium, sodium, chloride, bicarbonate and other dissolved ions.

These ions can contribute to the overall salinity of the water and can also influence nutrient management.

The mineral composition of source water should be considered when developing a fertiliser program.

## Salinity

Excess soluble salts can reduce water availability to plants.

High salt concentrations can also cause direct injury to sensitive plants.

Symptoms of salt stress can include reduced growth, leaf-edge injury and other signs of plant stress.

The severity of salt effects depends on the crop, growing medium, irrigation conditions and concentration of salts.

## Sodium

Sodium can accumulate in growing media and can affect plant growth when present at excessive concentrations.

Sodium can also contribute to overall salinity.

The concentration of sodium in irrigation water should therefore be considered when evaluating long-term water suitability.

## Chloride

Chloride is another ion that can contribute to salinity.

Excessive chloride concentrations can cause toxicity in sensitive crops.

The suitability of chloride concentrations depends on the crop and production system.

## Irrigation considerations

Water-quality problems can become more important when irrigation water is repeatedly applied and salts accumulate in the root environment.

Good irrigation management can help reduce excessive salt accumulation.

The characteristics of the growing medium, drainage and irrigation volume all influence salt movement.

## Water testing

Laboratory testing can be used to determine the chemical composition of irrigation water.

A complete water analysis can provide information about EC, pH, alkalinity and individual mineral concentrations.

Testing provides a more complete assessment than relying on a single measurement such as EC.

## Hydroponic relevance

Water quality is particularly important in hydroponic systems because the source water becomes part of the nutrient solution.

Source-water minerals contribute to the final nutrient concentrations.

Alkalinity can influence pH management.

EC can contribute to the measured conductivity of the nutrient solution before fertilisers are added.

## Relevance to Leafy AI

This source provides general water-quality knowledge relevant to hydroponic and controlled production.

It supports explanations of EC, pH, alkalinity, salinity and source-water mineral composition.

The source is primarily written for nursery production rather than sweet basil hydroponics.

Its general water-quality concepts can support sensor interpretation, but project-approved basil operating limits should take precedence over generic water-quality guidance.
