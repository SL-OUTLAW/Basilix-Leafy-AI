# EnviroVeg - Hydroponic, Greenhouse and Organic Production

## Environmental management in vegetable production

Environmental management is an important component of sustainable vegetable production.

Production practices can be designed to improve the efficient use of water, nutrients, energy and other farm inputs.

Good environmental management also reduces the risk of pollution and unnecessary resource loss.

## Hydroponic production

Hydroponic systems allow water and nutrients to be delivered directly to crops.

Because hydroponic systems can recirculate water and nutrients, careful monitoring is important to maintain suitable growing conditions.

Water quality, nutrient concentration and system performance should be checked regularly.

## Water management

Efficient water use is an important component of environmentally responsible production.

Irrigation systems should be designed to deliver water efficiently and uniformly.

Water losses can occur through evaporation, runoff, leakage and inefficient application.

Monitoring irrigation performance can help identify opportunities to reduce unnecessary water use.

## Nutrient management

Fertiliser should be applied according to crop requirements.

Excess nutrient application can increase production costs and may contribute to environmental contamination.

Nutrient management should consider crop demand, water quality and the growing system.

In hydroponic systems, nutrient concentrations can change as crops absorb water and individual nutrients.

Monitoring and adjustment can therefore be important for maintaining an appropriate nutrient balance.

## Recycling and reuse

Recycling water and nutrients can improve resource efficiency.

Closed or partially closed production systems can reduce the amount of nutrient-rich water leaving the production area.

Recycling systems require appropriate monitoring and management to prevent nutrient imbalance and the spread of pathogens.

## Greenhouse management

Greenhouse systems can provide greater control over environmental conditions.

Management of ventilation, temperature and humidity can improve crop production and reduce environmental risks.

Energy use should also be considered when selecting and operating greenhouse heating, cooling and ventilation systems.

## Crop hygiene

Good hygiene practices reduce the risk of pests and diseases spreading through a production system.

Equipment and production areas should be maintained in a clean condition.

Movement of contaminated plant material, growing media, water and equipment between production areas should be minimised.

## Waste management

Waste materials should be managed to reduce environmental impact.

Organic material may be reused or composted where appropriate.

Waste-water containing nutrients should be managed so that it does not create environmental problems.

## Sustainable production practices

Sustainable production involves balancing productivity with efficient use of natural resources.

Important considerations include water efficiency, nutrient efficiency, energy use, waste management and protection of surrounding environments.

Record keeping and monitoring can help growers evaluate the effectiveness of production practices.

## Monitoring and continual improvement

Environmental and production practices should be reviewed regularly.

Monitoring resource use can help identify areas where efficiency can be improved.

Growers can use observations and production records to adjust practices over time.
