# Basil, A Summer Favorite

## Basil types

Basil is an annual herb commonly grown for culinary use.

Sweet basil, Ocimum basilicum, is the most common type.

Other basil types include purple basil, lemon basil, lime basil and Thai basil.

Genovese basil is a popular sweet-basil cultivar used for culinary purposes such as pesto and bruschetta.

Other sweet-basil cultivars include large-leaf types such as Napoletano and Italian Large Leaf, and compact small-leaf types such as Spicy Globe and Green Globe.

## Growing conditions

Basil requires warm growing conditions.

It grows best in full sun, with approximately six to eight hours of sunlight each day.

Basil should be planted after the danger of frost has passed and after the growing medium has warmed.

Basil seeds germinate at approximately 70°F.

Young plants are sensitive to cold temperatures and should not be rushed into cold growing conditions.

Basil can be grown in garden beds or containers using a fertile, well-draining growing medium.

## Water management

The growing medium should remain moist during active growth.

Water should be directed toward the growing medium rather than directly onto the leaves.

Mulching can help conserve moisture and reduce weed growth in conventional growing conditions.

## Pruning and plant maintenance

Once seedlings have developed their first six leaves, pruning can be used to encourage branching.

Branches can be repeatedly pruned as they develop.

Flower buds should be removed because allowing basil to flower can reduce leaf quality and flavour.

Regular harvesting encourages continued production and helps maintain a bushy plant.

## Harvesting

Basil leaves can be harvested regularly throughout the growing season.

Frequent harvesting encourages continued leaf production.

Basil should be harvested before cold weather because low temperatures can discolor the leaves.

## Pests and diseases

Downy mildew is identified as an important basil disease.

Symptoms include yellowing leaves and gray, downy growth on the undersides of affected leaves.

Downy mildew can spread through airborne spores.

Cultural practices that reduce leaf wetness and humidity can help discourage disease development.

Good sunlight and air movement can reduce conditions favourable to the disease.

Overhead watering should be avoided where possible.

Sweet-basil varieties are described as particularly susceptible to downy mildew, while some other basil types may show lower susceptibility.

The source provides general basil growth and plant-health knowledge that can support explanations about basil development and disease conditions.

The growing recommendations are primarily based on conventional garden and container production rather than NFT or hydroponic production.
