# Hydroponics Systems: Using the Two Basic Equations to Calculate a Nutrient Solution Recipe

## Purpose

The two basic equations can be used to calculate the amounts of individual fertilizers required to prepare a hydroponic nutrient solution recipe.

The calculations can be applied to a complete nutrient recipe containing macronutrients and micronutrients.

## Example nutrient solution

The worked example uses a Modified Sonneveld solution for herbs grown in an NFT system.

The target nutrient concentrations in the recipe are:

Nitrogen: 150 ppm

Phosphorus: 31 ppm

Potassium: 210 ppm

Calcium: 90 ppm

Magnesium: 24 ppm

Iron: 1 ppm

Manganese: 0.25 ppm

Zinc: 0.13 ppm

Copper: 0.023 ppm

Boron: 0.16 ppm

Molybdenum: 0.024 ppm

The example uses two 5-gallon stock tanks with injectors set at a 100:1 concentration.

## Fertilizers used

The example uses calcium nitrate, ammonium nitrate, potassium nitrate, potassium phosphate monobasic, magnesium sulfate, Sequestrene 330, manganese sulfate, zinc sulfate, copper sulfate, borax and sodium molybdate.

Some fertilizers supply more than one nutrient.

Other fertilizers primarily supply a single nutrient.

## Calculating calcium

Calcium nitrate is used to supply the calcium requirement because it is the selected calcium source in the recipe.

The calculation determines that approximately 895.3 grams of calcium nitrate are needed to supply 90 ppm calcium under the conditions of the example.

Calcium nitrate also supplies nitrogen.

The calculated contribution is approximately 73.4 ppm nitrogen.

Because the recipe requires 150 ppm nitrogen, an additional 76.6 ppm nitrogen is still required after accounting for the nitrogen supplied by calcium nitrate.

## Calculating phosphorus and potassium

Potassium phosphate monobasic is used to supply the phosphorus requirement.

The calculation determines that approximately 262 grams are required to supply 31 ppm phosphorus.

This fertilizer also contributes potassium.

The calculated potassium contribution is approximately 39 ppm.

Because the recipe requires 210 ppm potassium, an additional 171 ppm potassium is required.

Potassium nitrate is then used to supply the remaining potassium requirement.

Approximately 885 grams of potassium nitrate are required to provide the remaining 171 ppm potassium.

Potassium nitrate also contributes nitrogen.

The calculated nitrogen contribution is approximately 61 ppm.

After accounting for nitrogen from calcium nitrate and potassium nitrate, approximately 15.6 ppm nitrogen remains to be supplied.

## Calculating remaining nitrogen

Ammonium nitrate is used to supply the remaining nitrogen requirement.

The calculation determines that approximately 86.7 grams of ammonium nitrate are needed to provide the remaining 15.6 ppm nitrogen.

At this stage the nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and calcium requirements in the example have been accounted for.

## Micronutrients

The remaining nutrients can be calculated using the first basic equation when the selected fertilizer supplies the required nutrient.

The example requires approximately:

498.5 grams of magnesium sulfate for 24 ppm magnesium.

18.9 grams of Sequestrene 330 for 1 ppm iron.

1.5 grams of manganese sulfate for 0.25 ppm manganese.

692 milligrams of zinc sulfate for 0.13 ppm zinc.

174 milligrams of copper sulfate for 0.023 ppm copper.

2749 milligrams of borax for 0.16 ppm boron.

116 milligrams of sodium molybdate for 0.024 ppm molybdenum.

Small micronutrient quantities may require accurate weighing equipment.

## Stock tank organisation

The example uses two stock tanks because some fertilizers should be separated to reduce the risk of precipitation.

Calcium should generally be kept in a different stock tank from sulfates and phosphates because combinations of these compounds can form precipitates.

Precipitation can clog irrigation equipment such as emitters.

In the example, calcium nitrate can be placed in one stock tank while potassium phosphate monobasic, magnesium sulfate, manganese sulfate, zinc sulfate and copper sulfate are placed in the other.

The remaining fertilizers can be distributed between the stock tanks as appropriate.

## Accounting for nutrients in irrigation water

The nutrient content of the irrigation water should be considered when preparing a nutrient solution.

For example, if the irrigation water already contains 10 ppm magnesium and the target recipe requires 24 ppm magnesium, only an additional 14 ppm magnesium needs to be supplied by fertilizer.

This approach can reduce unnecessary fertilizer inputs and allow nutrient programs to be adjusted to the characteristics of the water source.

## Limitations of recipe calculations

Not every fertilizer combination will produce simple calculations.

A fertilizer that supplies more than one nutrient may provide more of one nutrient than the recipe requires while meeting another nutrient requirement.

In such cases, the remaining nutrient balance may need to be adjusted by changing fertilizer selection or accepting an additional contribution.
