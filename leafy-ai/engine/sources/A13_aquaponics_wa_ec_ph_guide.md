# EC and pH Guide for Hydroponics

## Electrical conductivity

Electrical conductivity is a measurement used to indicate the concentration of dissolved salts in a hydroponic nutrient solution.

EC can be used as a general indicator of nutrient-solution strength.

A higher EC indicates a greater concentration of dissolved ions.

EC does not identify the concentration of individual nutrients.

## Monitoring EC

EC should be measured regularly when managing a hydroponic nutrient solution.

Changes in EC can indicate changes in water and nutrient uptake.

A falling EC may occur when plants absorb nutrients from the solution more rapidly than water.

A rising EC may occur when plants absorb water more rapidly than nutrients or when salts accumulate.

EC should therefore be interpreted together with plant condition and other nutrient-solution measurements.

## pH

pH indicates the acidity or alkalinity of the nutrient solution.

Hydroponic crops require the nutrient solution to remain within a suitable pH range for nutrient availability.

pH should be monitored regularly because it can change as plants absorb nutrients.

Adjustments should be made carefully to avoid large or rapid changes.

## Basil

Basil is a crop for which hydroponic EC and pH can be monitored to support plant growth.

The guide provides practical reference values for basil hydroponic production.

These values are supplementary commercial or industry guidance.

They should not be treated as universal or project-approved Leafy AI operating limits.

## Nutrient-solution management

EC and pH should be monitored together when managing a hydroponic nutrient solution.

EC provides information about total dissolved salts.

pH provides information relevant to nutrient availability.

Neither measurement provides a complete nutrient analysis.

Where plant symptoms or abnormal readings occur, further investigation of individual nutrient concentrations and water quality may be required.

## Water quality

The quality of the source water influences nutrient-solution conditions.

Source-water EC contributes to the final EC of the nutrient solution.

Dissolved minerals and alkalinity can also influence nutrient management and pH behaviour.

Water quality should therefore be considered when preparing and maintaining hydroponic nutrient solutions.

## Adjustment

Nutrient-solution adjustments should be made gradually and with appropriate measurements.

Large additions of concentrated fertiliser or pH-adjustment chemicals can produce rapid changes in solution conditions.

Measurements should be taken after sufficient mixing to obtain a representative reading.

## Monitoring and record keeping

Regular records of EC and pH can help identify trends over time.

A trend can be more informative than a single measurement.

Changes in EC or pH should be considered together with crop growth, water consumption and nutrient management.
