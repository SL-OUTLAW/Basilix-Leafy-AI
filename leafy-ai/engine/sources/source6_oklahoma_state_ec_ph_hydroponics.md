## Hydroponics

Hydroponics is a method of crop production in which plants are grown without soil and nutrients are supplied through liquid nutrient solution.

Plant roots may be supported by artificial substrates such as perlite, vermiculite, rock wool, expanded clay, coconut coir, wooden fiber, or mixtures of substrates.

## Water quality and nutrient management

Water should be analyzed before starting hydroponic production. Testing should include pH, electrical conductivity (EC) and alkalinity.

Poor-quality water can cause nutrient toxicity or deficiency problems.

Water naturally contains salts such as sodium, calcium, magnesium bicarbonates, chlorides and sulfates. These salts can affect the EC and pH of nutrient solutions.

Acceptable water concentrations reported in the guide include sodium below 50 ppm, calcium below 150 ppm, magnesium bicarbonate below 50 ppm, chloride below 140 ppm and sulfate below 100 ppm.

Nutrient management in hydroponics focuses on using nutrients efficiently while maintaining plant productivity and avoiding environmental harm.

Four important characteristics for nutrient management in soilless culture are total salt concentration, pH, alkalinity and nutrient concentration ratio.

## Electrical conductivity and total salt concentration

Electrical conductivity (EC) reflects the concentration of dissolved salts in a nutrient solution.

When salts dissolve in water, they separate into ions that conduct electricity. A higher EC indicates a higher salt concentration, while a lower EC indicates a lower salt concentration.

Excessively high nutrient concentrations can cause osmotic stress, ion toxicity and nutrient imbalance.

Excessively low concentrations can contribute to nutrient deficiencies and reduced plant growth.

Conductivity Factor (CF) expresses electrical conductivity in mS/cm.

## pH and nutrient availability

pH measures how acidic or basic a solution is.

The pH of a nutrient solution affects nutrient availability and should be maintained within an appropriate range.

Nutrient solutions used for soilless culture should have a pH between 5 and 6, usually around 5.5, so that the pH in the root environment is maintained between 6 and 6.5.

## Alkalinity

Alkalinity refers to the concentration of bicarbonate or carbonate in water.

Bicarbonate is alkaline and can increase the pH of nutrient solutions.

High-alkalinity water above 75 ppm can cause nutrient-solution pH to increase.

When high-alkalinity water is used, nutrient-solution pH should be checked more frequently.

Alkalinity can be reduced by using an acidic fertiliser, adding an acid such as phosphoric acid, citric acid or vinegar, or using reverse osmosis.

## Basil EC and pH range

The guide provides optimum EC and pH ranges for different hydroponic crops.

For basil, the reported optimum range is:

EC: 1.0–1.6 mS/cm

pH: 5.5–6.0

## EC management

EC can be checked using an EC meter.

An EC meter should be calibrated using an appropriate EC buffer solution.

EC management involves filling the nutrient tank with water and adding fertiliser according to manufacturer recommendations.

The EC meter should be calibrated before measurement.

The nutrient solution should be mixed thoroughly and allowed to stabilise before reading.

If EC is higher than the optimum level, the solution should be diluted with additional water and rechecked.

If EC is below the optimum level, nutrient concentrate should be added until the desired EC is reached.

The EC probe should be rinsed and stored according to the meter manufacturer's instructions.

## pH management

pH is measured using a pH meter.

The pH meter probe should be calibrated using pH buffer solutions.

The nutrient solution should be stirred and allowed to stabilise before taking the reading.

If pH is too high, phosphoric acid, citric acid, vinegar or a pH-down product can be added gradually. The solution should be allowed to stabilise before further adjustment.

If pH is too low, potassium hydroxide, potassium carbonate or a pH-up product can be added gradually.

The probe should be cleaned and stored in cleaning solution after use.

## Monitoring practices

The pH should be checked after EC has been adjusted to the optimum range.

The guide recommends checking both pH and EC daily.

Measurements should be taken at approximately the same time of day to improve consistency.

## Refreshing nutrient solution

Water may need to be added daily to replace water consumed by plants.

Nutrient ratios can move outside desired limits over time, which can result in nutrient deficiencies or toxicities.

The guide gives sodium chloride accumulation as an example of a potential toxicity problem.

Complete replacement of the nutrient solution is recommended approximately every two weeks.

## Automated monitoring

Manual pH and EC adjustment can be practical for small-scale operations.

For commercial farms, the volume of nutrient solution can make manual monitoring time-consuming.

Automated monitoring systems can reduce labour requirements, help avoid nutrient shock to plants and reduce human error.
