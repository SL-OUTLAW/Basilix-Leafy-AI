# Basil Downy Mildew

## Disease cause

Basil downy mildew is a plant disease caused by an oomycete pathogen.

The disease can affect basil leaves and may develop rapidly under favourable environmental conditions.

## Symptoms

The first visible symptoms can include pale green or yellow areas on the upper surfaces of basil leaves.

As symptoms develop, the affected areas can become larger and more clearly defined.

A gray, purple or brown fuzzy growth may develop on the underside of affected leaves.

Severely affected leaves may turn brown and fall from the plant.

Disease development can reduce basil quality and marketability.

## Environmental conditions

Basil downy mildew is favoured by humid conditions.

Extended periods of leaf wetness can increase the likelihood of disease development.

Warm conditions combined with high humidity can create favourable conditions for infection and disease spread.

## Disease spread

The pathogen can produce spores that are spread through air movement.

Spores can move between plants and production areas.

The disease can therefore spread rapidly when susceptible basil is grown under favourable environmental conditions.

## Monitoring

Regular inspection of basil plants is important for early recognition.

Both the upper and lower surfaces of leaves should be examined.

Yellowing on the upper leaf surface together with gray or fuzzy growth on the underside is characteristic of basil downy mildew.

Environmental conditions should also be monitored because disease risk is strongly influenced by humidity and leaf wetness.

## Cultural management

Reducing periods of high humidity can help reduce disease risk.

Good air circulation can improve drying of plant foliage.

Irrigation should be managed to avoid unnecessary leaf wetness.

Overhead irrigation should be avoided where practical because it can increase leaf wetness.

Growing plants with adequate spacing can improve airflow around foliage.

## Resistant varieties

Some basil varieties have greater resistance to downy mildew than others.

Using resistant varieties can be part of an integrated disease-management approach.

Resistance does not necessarily provide complete protection and should be combined with environmental and cultural management.

## Integrated disease management

Disease management can combine resistant varieties, environmental management, regular monitoring and sanitation.

Reducing humidity and leaf wetness can make environmental conditions less favourable for disease development.

Early detection can help limit spread within a production area.

## Distinguishing disease from other problems

Basil downy mildew symptoms can resemble some nutritional disorders because both can cause leaf yellowing.

Inspection of the underside of the leaves is important when distinguishing downy mildew from nutrient-related problems.

Environmental information such as humidity and leaf wetness can also support interpretation of plant symptoms.

Plant symptoms alone should not be treated as definitive evidence of a particular disease.
