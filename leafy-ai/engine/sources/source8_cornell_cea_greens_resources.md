# Controlled Environment Agriculture - Greens Resources

## Controlled-environment agriculture

Controlled-environment agriculture uses protected growing environments in which environmental conditions can be monitored and managed to support crop production.

Important growing conditions can include light, temperature, humidity, carbon dioxide, irrigation and nutrient availability.

Hydroponic production is one method used within controlled-environment agriculture.

## Hydroponic greens

Leafy greens can be produced using controlled-environment hydroponic systems.

Hydroponic systems can provide precise control of water and nutrient delivery to plant roots.

Environmental management is important because crop growth and quality depend on the interaction between light, temperature, water and nutrients.

## Lighting

Light is a major factor affecting growth and productivity in controlled-environment crops.

The quantity and duration of light available to plants influence photosynthesis and crop development.

Supplemental lighting can be used when natural light is insufficient.

Plant-level light measurements are useful when evaluating whether a controlled-environment crop is receiving an appropriate amount of light.

## Environmental control

Temperature and humidity affect plant growth, transpiration and disease development.

Air movement can improve environmental uniformity and reduce excessively humid conditions around plant foliage.

Carbon dioxide concentration can also influence photosynthesis in controlled environments.

Monitoring environmental variables allows growers to identify changes that may affect crop growth.

## Water and nutrient management

Hydroponic crops depend on the nutrient solution as the primary source of mineral nutrients.

Important nutrient-solution variables include pH and electrical conductivity.

Water quality can influence nutrient availability and the effectiveness of a hydroponic nutrient program.

Nutrient-solution management should therefore consider both the nutrient formulation and the characteristics of the source water.

## Plant health

Controlled-environment crops can still experience plant diseases and physiological disorders.

Disease risk is affected by environmental conditions such as humidity, temperature and leaf wetness.

Regular monitoring can help identify developing crop-health problems.

Plant symptoms should be interpreted together with environmental and nutrient information rather than using a single symptom as definitive evidence of a cause.

## Basil relevance

The Cornell controlled-environment agriculture resource collection includes information relevant to hydroponic greens and basil production.

Basil-specific knowledge can include nutrition, disease identification and environmental management.

Because the resource is a broad collection rather than a single basil-specific production protocol, information from more specific basil and hydroponic sources should be preferred when exact operating values are required.
