# Hydroponic Lettuce Handbook

## Hydroponic production system

The Cornell Controlled Environment Agriculture hydroponic lettuce system was designed for small-scale local production of head lettuce.

The system uses a floating hydroponic growing method in which plants are supported on floating rafts over a nutrient solution.

The production system was designed for consistent year-round production.

Consistent production requires substantial environmental control, including management of light, temperature, humidity, carbon dioxide, dissolved oxygen, pH and electrical conductivity.

## Environmental monitoring

Important environmental variables include air temperature, water temperature, relative humidity, carbon dioxide concentration, light, dissolved oxygen, pH and electrical conductivity.

Sensors can be used to continuously monitor these conditions.

A quantum PAR sensor can be used to measure light available for photosynthesis.

Light measurements intended for human vision, such as foot-candle measurements and standard lux meters, are not appropriate substitutes for measurements of photosynthetically active radiation.

Dissolved oxygen is important for hydroponic root health.

The handbook reports that dissolved oxygen should remain above 4 ppm to prevent growth inhibition, with visible plant stress potentially occurring around 3 ppm.

## Lighting

Supplemental lighting is used to provide sufficient light for plant growth when natural light is inadequate.

The lighting system can be controlled using measurements of plant-level irradiance.

The handbook describes high-pressure sodium and metal-halide lamps as supplemental lighting options used in the production system.

Uniform light distribution is important for consistent crop production.

The example lettuce production system used a total daily light integral of approximately 17 mol m-2 d-1 from natural and supplemental lighting.

The lighting requirement depends on crop variety and environmental conditions.

The handbook notes that excessive light can contribute to physiological disorders such as tipburn in susceptible lettuce cultivars.

## Air movement and humidity

Air movement is used to increase plant transpiration.

A vertical airflow system can increase transpiration and improve movement of nutrients such as calcium from roots to young leaves.

In lettuce, improved calcium transport can help reduce tipburn.

Relative humidity should be controlled as part of the greenhouse environment.

The example production system targeted relative humidity between approximately 50% and 70%.

## Temperature

The example lettuce system used a target air temperature of approximately 24°C during the day and 19°C at night.

Water temperature was also controlled.

The example system maintained water temperature below approximately 25°C and used active cooling or heating when required.

These values are described for the specific lettuce production system and should not be treated as universal targets for basil.

## Carbon dioxide

Carbon dioxide concentration can be controlled as part of a controlled-environment production system.

The example system used elevated carbon dioxide when sufficient light was available and returned to approximately ambient concentration when light was unavailable.

Carbon dioxide enrichment should therefore be considered together with light availability and the specific crop response.

## pH and electrical conductivity

The hydroponic nutrient solution was monitored for pH and electrical conductivity.

The example lettuce production system used a pH range of approximately 5.6 to 6.0.

The reported electrical conductivity target was approximately 1150–1250 microsiemens per centimetre above the conductivity of the source water.

These values are specific to the Cornell lettuce system and should not be interpreted as approved basil operating limits.

## Dissolved oxygen

Dissolved oxygen can be monitored directly in the nutrient solution.

Adequate oxygen availability is important for healthy root function.

The Cornell system identified approximately 7 mg/L as a target dissolved oxygen level, while concentrations below approximately 3 mg/L were associated with severe growth problems.

## Production stages

The lettuce production cycle begins in a germination area.

The example production schedule uses approximately 11 days for germination and early plant development.

Plants are then transferred to the greenhouse and transplanted into the pond growing area.

Plants are re-spaced as they grow and are eventually harvested.

The example production schedule reaches harvest at approximately day 35.

Production timing depends on cultivar, environmental conditions and the management system.

## Monitoring and control

Controlled-environment hydroponic production requires regular monitoring of environmental and nutrient conditions.

Automated controls can adjust supplemental lighting and environmental systems based on sensor measurements.

Monitoring allows growers to maintain conditions within desired ranges and identify changes that may affect crop growth.

## Relevance to Leafy AI

The handbook is focused on lettuce rather than basil.

Its numerical setpoints are examples from a specific lettuce production system and should not be used as Leafy AI basil operating limits.
