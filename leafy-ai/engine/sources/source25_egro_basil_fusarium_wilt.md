# Basil Fusarium Wilt

## Disease cause

Basil Fusarium wilt is caused by the soilborne fungus Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. basilicum.

The pathogen specifically affects basil and can cause serious losses when susceptible plants are grown under favourable conditions.

## Symptoms

Symptoms can begin with reduced plant growth and uneven development.

Affected plants may become stunted.

Leaves can develop yellowing and may wilt even when adequate water is available.

Wilting may begin on one side of a plant and progress as the disease develops.

Older leaves can show symptoms before younger leaves.

In advanced cases, leaves may collapse and plants may die.

## Vascular symptoms

Discolouration of the vascular tissue is an important diagnostic feature.

When affected stems are cut open, the vascular tissue may appear brown or darkened.

Vascular discolouration can extend along the stem.

The pathogen interferes with the plant's vascular system and restricts normal water movement.

## Disease development

Fusarium wilt can persist in contaminated growing environments.

The pathogen can survive for long periods in soil or contaminated plant material.

Disease development can be favoured when susceptible basil plants are grown repeatedly in contaminated production areas.

## Disease spread

Fusarium wilt can spread through contaminated soil, growing media, water and equipment.

Movement of infected plant material can also contribute to spread.

Sanitation is important when managing production areas where the pathogen may be present.

Equipment and growing containers should be cleaned and disinfected between production cycles where appropriate.

## Diagnosis

Possible Fusarium wilt should be considered when basil shows progressive wilting, stunting and vascular discolouration.

Plant symptoms alone are not sufficient for definitive identification.

Laboratory testing may be required to confirm the pathogen.

Other causes of wilting, nutrient problems and root disorders should also be considered during diagnosis.

## Prevention and management

Using clean planting material can reduce the risk of introducing the pathogen.

Good sanitation practices can reduce movement of contaminated material between production areas.

Removing severely affected plants can help reduce the amount of infected material in the production environment.

Avoiding movement of contaminated growing media, water or equipment can reduce disease spread.

Crop hygiene and sanitation are important components of disease prevention.

## Resistant varieties

Use of basil varieties with resistance to Fusarium wilt can reduce disease risk where resistant varieties are available.

Resistance should be considered as part of an integrated management strategy.

## Hydroponic relevance

Fusarium wilt is not limited to conventional soil production.

The pathogen can affect basil grown in protected and hydroponic production systems when contaminated material or water introduces the pathogen.

Hydroponic systems should therefore maintain appropriate sanitation and hygiene practices.
