# Hydroponic Production Systems

## Hydroponic production

Hydroponic production systems grow plants without conventional soil and provide water and nutrients directly to the plant root environment.

Hydroponic systems can be used in protected and controlled production environments.

The major hydroponic system types differ in how plants are supported and how water and nutrients are delivered.

## Hydroponic system types

Hydroponic systems may be classified as liquid-culture systems or aggregate-culture systems.

In liquid-culture systems, plant roots are exposed directly to the nutrient solution.

In aggregate systems, plants are supported by a growing medium while nutrient solution is supplied to the root zone.

Examples of hydroponic systems include nutrient film technique, deep-water culture, ebb-and-flow systems and drip irrigation systems.

## Nutrient solution

The nutrient solution supplies mineral nutrients required for plant growth.

The composition of a nutrient solution should provide essential plant nutrients in suitable proportions.

Nutrient concentrations can change as plants absorb water and nutrients.

Regular monitoring and management are therefore important.

## Water quality

The quality of the source water affects hydroponic nutrient management.

Important water characteristics can include electrical conductivity, alkalinity, pH and the concentration of dissolved minerals.

Water containing high concentrations of certain minerals can affect nutrient formulation and plant nutrition.

Source-water characteristics should be considered before preparing a nutrient solution.

## Electrical conductivity

Electrical conductivity provides an indication of the total concentration of dissolved salts in a nutrient solution.

EC can be used as a general indicator of nutrient-solution concentration.

EC does not identify individual nutrients and should not be interpreted as a complete nutrient analysis.

Changes in EC over time can help indicate changes in the balance between water uptake and nutrient uptake.

## pH

Nutrient-solution pH influences the availability of mineral nutrients to plants.

Hydroponic systems generally require management of pH within a range suitable for crop growth.

pH can change as plants absorb nutrients and as nutrient solutions are replenished or adjusted.

Regular monitoring can help maintain suitable conditions.

## Nutrient delivery

Hydroponic systems can deliver nutrient solution continuously or intermittently depending on system design.

The frequency and volume of solution delivery should provide adequate moisture and nutrients while maintaining suitable root-zone conditions.

In systems using growing media, irrigation scheduling should account for the water-holding characteristics of the medium and the needs of the crop.

## Root-zone oxygen

Healthy roots require adequate oxygen.

In liquid hydroponic systems, dissolved oxygen can be particularly important because roots may be directly exposed to the nutrient solution.

Water temperature and circulation can influence dissolved oxygen availability.

Maintaining suitable oxygen conditions helps support root function.

## Irrigation and recirculation

Hydroponic systems may use recirculating nutrient solutions.

Recirculation can improve water and nutrient-use efficiency.

However, recirculating systems require monitoring because changes in nutrient concentration, pH, EC and plant health can affect the reused solution.

Pathogens can also spread through shared nutrient solution in recirculating systems.

## Monitoring

Hydroponic systems benefit from regular monitoring of nutrient-solution conditions and crop health.

Useful measurements can include pH, EC, water temperature and dissolved oxygen where appropriate.

Plant growth, leaf appearance and root condition should also be monitored.

Changes in measurements or plant condition can indicate the need for further investigation or adjustment.
