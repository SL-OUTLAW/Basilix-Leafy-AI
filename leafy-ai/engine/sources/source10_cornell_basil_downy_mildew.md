# Basil Downy Mildew






Updated: March 2023

## Overview

Basil downy mildew is caused by the oomycete pathogen Peronospora belbahrii.

The pathogen produces abundant spores that can be dispersed by wind.

The pathogen is also seed-borne.

Leaves showing symptoms can become unmarketable, particularly when basil is sold as a fresh herb.

Severe outbreaks can cause complete crop loss.

## Symptoms and signs

The most noticeable initial symptom is chlorosis, or yellowing of basil leaves.

Chlorosis can resemble a nutritional deficiency, which can make early diagnosis difficult.

As the disease develops, characteristic sporulation appears mainly on the lower surfaces of leaves.

Observing spores on the underside of leaves is an important diagnostic feature.

Spores are produced during the dark night period, so early morning is a useful time to inspect basil plants for downy mildew.

Affected leaves may show yellowing on the upper surface with corresponding purplish-gray sporulation on the lower surface.

The yellowing may occur in sections of the leaf separated by major veins.

Affected tissue can eventually die and become brown and necrotic.

Young seedlings can also be affected. Symptoms on newly emerged leaves and cotyledons may indicate systemic infection associated with contaminated seed, particularly when no nearby infected plants are present.

## Distinguishing downy mildew from other problems

Several other problems can produce symptoms resembling basil downy mildew.

Physiological injury and insect damage may cause leaf chlorosis or necrosis.

Sunburn or other physiological injury may resemble downy mildew but lacks the characteristic sporulation on the lower leaf surface.

Thrips injury can also be confused with downy mildew but lacks the dusty or fuzzy appearance of downy mildew sporulation.

Because nutrient deficiencies and other disorders can resemble downy mildew, diagnosis should consider symptoms on both sides of the leaf and the presence or absence of characteristic sporulation.

## Greenhouse management

Important management practices include using seed that is not infested with the pathogen, selecting less susceptible or resistant varieties, and using appropriate disease-management treatments.

For greenhouse production, pathogen-free seed is particularly important when crops are not expected to be exposed to wind-dispersed spores from outside sources.

Minimizing leaf wetness and reducing humidity can create conditions that are less favorable for disease development.

Regular crop scouting is important for detecting symptoms early.

## Resistant varieties

Resistant basil varieties can provide substantial suppression of downy mildew.

Examples of resistant varieties discussed by Cornell include several Rutgers-derived varieties such as Devotion, Obsession, Passion and Thunderstruck.

Prospera varieties were also developed with genetic resistance to basil downy mildew.

Amazel and Pesto Besto are additional resistant basil varieties discussed by Cornell.

Resistance is not equivalent to complete immunity, and some symptoms may still develop.

The effectiveness of resistance can vary between environments and seasons, and the pathogen may evolve in ways that reduce resistance effectiveness.

Resistant varieties should therefore be used together with other disease-management practices.

## Monitoring resistant varieties

Regular scouting is important even when resistant varieties are being used.

When symptoms are detected, an appropriate disease-management program can be initiated promptly.

Cornell notes that resistant varieties may need to be combined with other management practices because fresh-market basil has a low tolerance for visible disease symptoms.

## Environmental conditions and disease development

Disease development is influenced by environmental conditions.

Leaf wetness and high humidity can favor infection.

The basil downy mildew pathogen can develop under moderate to warm temperatures while also tolerating cool temperatures.

Periods with conditions favorable for infection and long-distance spore dispersal increase disease risk.

## Crop monitoring

Growers should regularly inspect basil crops for symptoms.

Early morning inspection is particularly useful because sporulation develops during the dark period.

Growers should look for yellowing on leaf surfaces and inspect the undersides of leaves for characteristic sporulation.

Monitoring local reports of downy mildew occurrence can also help growers assess disease risk.

## Key diagnostic principle

Chlorosis alone does not confirm basil downy mildew because nutrient deficiencies and other disorders can produce similar yellowing.

The presence of characteristic sporulation on the lower leaf surface is an important diagnostic feature.

When symptoms are uncertain, the crop should be examined carefully and other potential causes considered.
