# Fusarium Wilt and Base Rot of Basil







## Cause

Fusarium wilt and base rot of basil is caused by the fungus Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. basilicum.

## Host plant

Basil is the host plant described for this disease.

## Importance

Fusarium wilt and base rot is a major disease of basil in the Top End.

The disease occurs in both field-grown basil and hydroponic basil production.

## Symptoms

Seedlings may develop black, water-soaked patches on leaves and stems.

When stems and roots are dissected, the vascular tissue may appear grey-brown.

Symptoms in mature basil plants can include stunting, wilting and defoliation.

Outer stem tissue may become discoloured.

Lateral shoots may die back from the tips.

Dissected stems and roots may show dark grey to red-brown discolouration of vascular tissue.

In advanced disease, the stem pith can break down, resulting in base rot.

## Source and spread of infection

The disease was first introduced into Australia in infected seed.

Local spread can occur through infected soil.

Once introduced onto a property, Fusarium wilt can persist in soil for years.

Fungal spores can be spread by air currents, wind and water.

## Disease management

Use resistant basil varieties where appropriate.

Use certified disease-free seed for planting.

Avoid moving equipment from infected sites to uninfected sites.

For hydroponic production, trays and pots should be disinfected using an agricultural sanitiser.

Good hygiene and sanitation are important for preventing movement of the pathogen between production areas.

## Key diagnostic features

Important signs to consider when investigating suspected Fusarium wilt and base rot include:

Black, water-soaked lesions on seedlings.

Stunting, wilting and defoliation in mature plants.

Discolouration of vascular tissue inside stems and roots.

Dark grey to red-brown vascular discolouration.

Breakdown of stem pith and development of base rot in advanced disease.
