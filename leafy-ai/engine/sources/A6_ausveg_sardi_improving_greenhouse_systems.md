# Improving Greenhouse Systems and Production Practices

## Protected cropping

Protected cropping systems use structures such as greenhouses to provide greater control over the crop environment.

Environmental control can improve production consistency and reduce exposure to some external conditions.

Greenhouse production requires appropriate management of temperature, humidity, irrigation and crop nutrition.

## Greenhouse system planning

Successful greenhouse production depends on selecting systems and equipment that match the crop and production objectives.

Greenhouse design should consider crop requirements, climate, irrigation, ventilation, heating and cooling requirements.

System planning should also consider labour requirements and the movement of people and equipment through the production area.

## Hydroponic production

Hydroponic systems can be incorporated into protected cropping operations.

Hydroponic production provides opportunities for precise water and nutrient management.

Different hydroponic systems require different approaches to nutrient delivery, drainage and crop support.

System selection should be based on crop requirements and the intended production environment.

## Irrigation

Efficient irrigation is an important part of greenhouse crop production.

Irrigation scheduling should account for crop demand and environmental conditions.

Over-irrigation can waste water and nutrients, while under-irrigation can stress plants.

The irrigation system should provide uniform delivery to the crop.

## Nutrient management

Nutrient management is important for maintaining productive greenhouse crops.

Fertiliser inputs should be matched to crop requirements.

Water quality can affect nutrient management and should be considered when preparing irrigation or nutrient solutions.

Monitoring plant growth and nutrient conditions can help identify problems and improve management.

## Environmental management

Temperature and humidity are important greenhouse management variables.

Ventilation can be used to remove excess heat and humidity.

Heating may be required during cooler periods.

Greenhouse environmental systems should be selected and operated according to crop requirements and local climate conditions.

## Crop monitoring

Regular crop monitoring can identify changes in plant growth and crop health.

Monitoring should include plant appearance, growth and environmental conditions.

Early identification of problems can help growers adjust irrigation, nutrition or environmental conditions before crop losses become significant.

## Hygiene and system management

Good hygiene practices are important in protected-cropping systems.

Equipment, tools and production areas should be maintained to reduce the risk of spreading pests and diseases.

Management procedures should reduce unnecessary movement of contaminated material between production areas.

## Labour and production efficiency

Greenhouse production systems should be designed with labour efficiency in mind.

The arrangement of production areas, irrigation systems and crop rows can affect the time required for routine tasks.

Production planning should consider both biological requirements and the practical requirements of workers.

## Hydroponic conversion

Converting an existing greenhouse to hydroponic production requires consideration of the growing system, irrigation infrastructure, water supply, drainage and nutrient management.

The conversion should be planned around the crop and the available infrastructure.

Appropriate training is also important when introducing new production systems.
