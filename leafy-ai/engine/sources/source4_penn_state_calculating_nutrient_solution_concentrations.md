# Hydroponics Systems: Calculating Nutrient Solution Concentrations Using the Two Basic Equations

## Purpose

Calculating the amount of fertilizer needed for a hydroponic nutrient solution is an important part of hydroponic production.

The calculations use multiplication, division and subtraction.

Hydroponic fertilizer recipes are commonly expressed in parts per million (ppm).

For nutrient concentrations in water, 1 ppm is equivalent to 1 mg/L.

## Converting nutrient concentrations

When a nutrient recipe calls for a concentration such as 150 ppm nitrogen, this corresponds to 150 mg of nitrogen per litre of irrigation water.

Hydroponic nutrient recipes may specify phosphorus as elemental P and potassium as elemental K.

Fertilizer grades commonly express phosphorus as P2O5 and potassium as K2O.

P2O5 contains approximately 43% elemental phosphorus.

K2O contains approximately 83% elemental potassium.

These conversions must be considered when calculating how much fertilizer is required.

## Converting between measurement units

Hydroponic nutrient calculations may require conversions between litres and gallons.

The conversion used in the example is approximately 3.78 litres per US gallon.

Fertilizer quantities may also need to be converted between milligrams and grams.

There are 1,000 milligrams in one gram.

## Calculating fertilizer requirements

The first basic equation can be used to determine how much fertilizer is required to provide a specified nutrient concentration.

The calculation accounts for the desired nutrient concentration, the volume of water, the concentration of the stock solution and the percentage of the nutrient contained in the fertilizer.

For example, a nutrient solution requiring 150 ppm nitrogen can be calculated using a 20-20-20 fertilizer and a 100:1 stock-solution concentration.

For a five-gallon stock tank, the example calculation determines that approximately 1,417.5 grams of fertilizer are required.

The equivalent amount is approximately 3.12 pounds of fertilizer.

## Determining nutrients supplied by a fertilizer

The second basic equation can be used to determine how much of another nutrient is supplied by a fertilizer after the required amount of fertilizer has been calculated.

This can be used when a fertilizer supplies multiple nutrients.

In the example, a 20-20-20 fertilizer supplying 150 ppm nitrogen also supplies approximately 64.5 ppm phosphorus and 124.5 ppm potassium after the relevant nutrient conversions are applied.

The calculation accounts for the fertilizer concentration, stock-solution concentration and the conversion between fertilizer nutrient forms and elemental nutrients.

## Stock-solution calculations

A concentrated stock solution can be prepared for use with a fertilizer injector.

A concentration of 100:1 means that one part of stock solution is applied for every 100 parts of water delivered to the crop.

The stock-tank volume and injector concentration must be included when calculating the amount of fertilizer to add.

## Practical use

The two basic equations can be used to calculate nutrient concentrations for hydroponic nutrient-solution recipes.

The equations can also be used to determine the contribution of individual fertilizers to a complete nutrient program.

Accurate measurement of fertilizer quantities is important when preparing nutrient solutions.

High-precision scales may be required when calculating small quantities of micronutrients.
