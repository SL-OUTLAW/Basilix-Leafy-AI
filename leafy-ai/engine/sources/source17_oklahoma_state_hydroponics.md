# Hydroponics

## Hydroponic production

Hydroponics is a method of growing plants without soil by supplying water and mineral nutrients directly to the plant root environment.

Hydroponic production can be used in protected and controlled environments.

The main purpose of a hydroponic system is to provide plants with suitable water, oxygen and mineral nutrients while maintaining an appropriate root environment.

## Hydroponic system types

Hydroponic systems can be divided into systems where roots are supported by a growing medium and systems where roots are directly exposed to nutrient solution.

Examples include nutrient film technique, deep-water culture, ebb-and-flow systems and drip systems.

Each system has different water-delivery and root-zone characteristics.

## Nutrient solution

The nutrient solution supplies the mineral nutrients required for plant growth.

A complete nutrient solution should contain the essential elements needed by the crop.

Nutrient concentrations can change as plants absorb water and nutrients.

Regular monitoring helps maintain suitable nutrient-solution conditions.

## Water quality

Source-water quality is important in hydroponic production.

Water can contain dissolved salts and minerals that affect the final nutrient solution.

Electrical conductivity can be used to estimate the total concentration of dissolved salts.

The characteristics of source water should be considered when preparing nutrient solutions.

## pH

Nutrient-solution pH affects nutrient availability.

Most hydroponic crops require the nutrient solution to be maintained within a suitable pH range.

pH can change as nutrients are absorbed by plants or as water and fertilisers are added.

Regular pH measurement and adjustment can therefore be important.

## Electrical conductivity

Electrical conductivity provides an indication of the concentration of dissolved salts in a hydroponic solution.

EC is useful for monitoring changes in overall nutrient-solution concentration.

EC does not identify individual nutrients.

A suitable EC range depends on the crop and production system.

## Oxygen and root health

Plant roots require oxygen for respiration.

In water-based hydroponic systems, dissolved oxygen is particularly important because roots may remain in direct contact with nutrient solution.

Water temperature, aeration and circulation can affect dissolved oxygen.

Maintaining adequate oxygen supports healthy root development.

## Water and nutrient delivery

Hydroponic systems can deliver nutrient solution continuously or intermittently.

The delivery schedule should provide sufficient water and nutrients while maintaining suitable root-zone conditions.

Recirculating systems can improve water and nutrient-use efficiency.

However, recirculation also means that changes in nutrient concentration or plant health can affect the whole system.

## Temperature

Water and air temperature affect plant growth and root-zone conditions.

Warm nutrient solutions can hold less dissolved oxygen than cooler solutions.

Temperature management should therefore be considered together with root oxygen availability.

## Monitoring

Hydroponic systems should be monitored regularly.

Useful measurements can include pH, EC, water temperature and dissolved oxygen where appropriate.

Plant growth and appearance should also be observed.

Changes in measurements or plant condition can indicate that further investigation is needed.

## Sanitation

Good sanitation practices are important in hydroponic systems.

Shared water and equipment can allow pathogens to spread through a production system.

Cleaning and disinfection of equipment can reduce the risk of pathogen transfer.
