## Study context

The study investigated the use of urine-derived fertiliser as a nutrient source for hydroponic plant production.

The research compared urine-derived fertiliser with commercial fertilisers under different growing conditions.

The study included parsley, rhipsalis, coriander and basil.

The basil experiment provides Australian university research evidence relevant to indoor hydroponic basil production and nutrient-solution comparisons.

## Basil experimental system

Basil was identified as Ocimum basilicum.

Basil plants were cultivated for three months in a deep-water culture indoor hydroponic system called Hydrogarden.

The experiment was conducted at the environmental laboratory of the University of Technology Sydney.

Six Hydrogarden units were installed in total, with three units used for each plant type.

Each unit was supplied with a different nutrient solution.

Vermiculite was used as the growing medium.

The Hydrogarden units included built-in LED lighting.

The LED lighting was programmed to operate on a daylight cycle with automatic switching.

The basil experiment used deep-water culture rather than nutrient film technique.

Therefore, the results should be treated as evidence for general indoor hydroponic basil and nutrient-solution behaviour rather than as NFT-specific operating rules.

## Nutrient treatments

The basil experiment compared three nutrient treatments.

T1_3 was a commercial fertiliser called HydroBoost liquid fertiliser from Mr Fothergill's Australia.

T2_3 was UrVal fertiliser, a urine-derived fertiliser produced through biological nitrification and concentration by distillation.

T3_3 was a urine-based fertiliser with elevated nitrite concentration.

The elevated-nitrite treatment was produced by destabilising nitrification in the membrane bioreactor through ammonia shock loading.

The treatment solutions were diluted with deionised water to achieve approximately 350 mg total nitrogen per litre.

Basil and coriander germinated after approximately two weeks.

The respective nutrient solutions were replenished weekly during the experiment.

## Basil growth results

Basil biomass under the UrVal fertiliser treatment was lower than under the commercial fertiliser treatment.

Basil shoot and root measurements were collected using fresh and oven-dried biomass measurements.

Basil biomass under the commercial fertiliser treatment was 90.7 g, compared with 42.5 g under the UrVal fertiliser treatment.

The difference in basil biomass between these treatments was statistically significant.

Despite the biomass difference, basil stem length did not differ significantly between the commercial fertiliser and UrVal treatments.

Basil stem lengths ranged from approximately 26 to 27 cm under these two treatments.

The root-to-shoot ratio showed a statistically significant difference between the commercial fertiliser and UrVal treatments, with reported ratios of 0.13 and 0.10 respectively.

## Effect of elevated nitrite

The elevated-nitrite treatment had a strong negative effect on basil growth.

Basil biomass under the high-nitrite treatment was approximately 1.1 g compared with 90.7 g under the commercial fertiliser treatment.

Basil stem length under the high-nitrite treatment was approximately 2.2 cm.

The plants showed weak and stunted growth.

Leaves turned yellow under the high-nitrite treatment.

The study reported that a nitrite concentration of approximately 120 mg/L significantly affected plant growth because of its toxic effect.

The authors also noted that further research is required to establish the concentration threshold at which nitrite causes stunted plant growth.

## Nutrient composition

Total nitrogen concentrations in basil shoots under the commercial fertiliser and UrVal treatments ranged approximately from 4.2% to 5.1%.

The commercial fertiliser treatment produced higher nitrate concentrations in shoots than the UrVal treatment.

The reported nitrate concentration in shoots was approximately 1.8–2% under the commercial fertiliser treatment and approximately 0.4–0.6% under the UrVal treatment.

The study associated the difference with the nitrate contribution of the respective fertiliser formulations.

## Relevance to Leafy AI

This source provides Australian experimental evidence about indoor hydroponic cultivation of basil.

It is relevant to understanding how basil growth can respond to different nutrient solutions in a controlled indoor environment.

It also provides evidence that nutrient composition and potentially toxic compounds in nutrient solutions can substantially affect basil biomass and growth.

The built-in LED lighting and controlled indoor Hydrogarden system provide context for technology-assisted basil production.

The study does not establish project-approved EC, pH, lighting or nutrient operating limits for Leafy AI.

The results should not be treated as universal nutrient targets because the experiment used specific fertiliser formulations, a specific deep-water culture system and controlled experimental conditions.

This source is not an NFT-specific operating guide.
