## Hydroponics as a response to land and soil constraints

In vegetable production areas where soil quality is poor or properties are rented, growers may invest in relocatable infrastructure such as hydroponic benches and basic pump systems.

Hydroponic production can allow growers to use land that is unsuitable for conventional vegetable production while remaining close to markets and growers' homes.

Hydroponic systems can also be modular, portable and relocatable when growers need to move farms.

## Grower transition from soil production to NFT hydroponics

Kim Ngov, a member of the Australian Cambodian Growers Association of NSW, had an interest in protected cropping and was passionate about growing basil hydroponically in a greenhouse environment.

In 2023, Kim moved from a rental property in Wedderburn to a family property in Kemps Creek, NSW. Much of the new property was unsuitable for field-grown vegetables because of poor drainage, flooding risk and high salinity.

Kim therefore transitioned from traditional soil-based farming to hydroponics, drawing on previous experience growing basil in protected cropping systems.

## NFT system setup

At the new farm, weed matting was laid under the benches for weed suppression while maintaining soil aeration.

Nutrient Film Technique (NFT) benches were installed to support the hydroponic systems.

The growing area was divided into bays, with each bay equipped with a 1000 L tank to capture runoff from the crop.

Each tank had a small submersible pond pump to recirculate the nutrient solution.

The modular setup helped reduce installation costs and minimised potential crop loss in the event of a pump failure.

The pumps were relatively inexpensive and widely available from major hardware stores.

## Grower education and study tours

VegNET NSW organised a bus tour of NFT hydroponic enterprises in Southwest Sydney in September 2025.

The purpose was to demonstrate NFT systems, good growing practices and protected cropping approaches to growers who were still using conventional field production.

The tour included visits to a vegetable production nursery and a hydroponic lettuce farm, where growers could observe different growing systems, infrastructure and irrigation practices.

Two participating growers later joined a WaterWise study tour for North Coast and Greater Sydney growers and irrigators to Northern NSW and Queensland in October 2025.

The study tours provided opportunities for growers and agronomists to observe hydroponic and protected-cropping systems and share practical knowledge.
